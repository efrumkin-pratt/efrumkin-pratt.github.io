Evolution of Home Media

Origins

Home media has become a part of a film's life starting in the late '70s following the blockbusters Jaws (1975) and Star Wars (1977). Before that movies would go from cinemas to tv channels when they're broadcast live. Before the invention of the VCR in the 1960s, this was the only way to see movies after its theatrical release.

In the 1970s, studios recognized the strength of video casette tapes for their catalog to be played at home and schools. Soon, companies like The Nostalgic Merchant and Magnetic Video secured rights from RKO and Fox's catalog, respectively.

Although early cassettes sold from $50 - $70, back when the average film ticket was $2.33, video distributors lowered the price to $15 - $20 and constructed video stores amidst the home market boom. Later, studios like Fox (who acquired the now defunt-Magnetic Video) saw the boom in the home video market and made it a priority to develop a home video division.

Home Media Benefits

  • Streaming
  • Accessibility
  • Cost-Efficient

It takes a lot out of people, financially and logistically, to go to the cinema.

Recent Studies has shown that Americans and Canadians have bought over $792 million in ticket sales, roughly 2 per person .

With more films being available through on demand services and website such as YouTube and Vimeo, home media is at its strongest today.

Home Media Cons

  • Short shell-life
    • Some modern computers do not have a disc slot in their design. DVDs, if they're not properly stored, can lose durability through potential buffers.
  • Limited Audience Reach
    • There's limited copies of media when they are sold at retail stores and to borrow at a rental or library.
  • Piracy Concerns
    • Physical media is vulnerable to piracy when the film lands on the people's hands. Revenue decreases and there's a possibility that file transfer will be uploaded unauthroized on a disc, VHS, or streaming service.
